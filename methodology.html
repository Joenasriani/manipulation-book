Method & source standards

This page defines the claims the edition is making about its research process, and equally important, the claims it is not making.

Publication status

Manipulation — The Fool and the Wise is an independently published study edition by J. Nasr. It is not represented as a peer-reviewed journal article, academic monograph from a university press, clinical manual, or systematic review.

The edition is designed as a structured reference system: documented mechanisms are connected to source anchors; editorial syntheses and hypotheses are labeled separately; examples are teaching illustrations unless identified as sourced cases.

What “source-mapped” means here

“Source-mapped” means that the book attempts to connect each substantive mechanism or category to traceable source records rather than presenting the taxonomy as self-validating. It does not mean that every item has equal evidential strength, that every effect replicates universally, or that the book itself constitutes new empirical validation.

E — documented

A source record identifies a directly documented mechanism, established research construct, or authoritative legal/technical category relevant to the entry.

C — editorial synthesis

The exact label or configuration is assembled from documented components. The synthesis is the book's organization, not a claim that the combined label is itself an established scientific construct.

H — hypothesis

The precise configuration remains conjectural or insufficiently established and is kept separate from documented mechanisms.

Class 1 / Class 0

These are project-level implementation categories. They are not diagnoses, personality labels, or universal legal judgments.

Boundary rules

The edition treats boundaries as part of the evidence problem. Similar-looking events are not automatically classified together. A misleading outcome does not by itself prove manipulative intent; social influence is not automatically coercion; a third party is not automatically triangulation; a technical AI attack is not automatically psychological persuasion.

This negative-ontology approach is used to reduce overclassification and to keep observation separate from inference.

Use of “dark psychology”

The phrase dark psychology is used only as a reader-facing umbrella term because people commonly search for material on manipulation, deception, coercion, exploitative influence and related behaviors under that label. The book does not treat “dark psychology” as one unified scientific discipline.

AI-assisted production disclosure

AI tools assisted parts of research organization, comparison, drafting, editing, coding and publication production. AI output is not treated as evidence. Claims are intended to stand or fall on the cited sources, the distinction between evidence and synthesis, and the stated limitations.

Rapid software or page iteration should not be confused with peer review, and it should not be used as a substitute for source verification. The public website is intentionally separated from private production notes, credentials, customer data and operational material for ordinary security and privacy reasons.

For psychology and behavioral-science readers

The relevant questions are whether constructs are correctly named, whether source claims match the cited literature, whether alternative explanations are acknowledged, and whether the text avoids turning an observed outcome into an unsupported claim about intent or personality.

Useful source domains include social and cognitive psychology, behavioral science, philosophy of manipulation, communication research, law and regulation, and human-computer interaction where interface design or digital choice architecture is involved.

For AI, HCI and human-factors readers

The book's AI layer should be read as an application and reasoning layer, not as evidence that a psychological mechanism automatically produces a viable AI product. The edition separates three questions: whether a phenomenon is documented; whether software could meaningfully detect, model or respond to it; and whether any proposed product has technical or market validation.

This distinction is intended to make the material useful for AI product research, human-AI interaction, safety, persuasive-technology analysis, interface ethics and behavioral evaluation without converting academic citations into unsupported product claims.

Public AI / HCI research routes

These pages extend the book's boundary and evidence framework into public, source-linked material for AI builders, red-teamers, HCI researchers and human-factors readers. They are editorial research routes, not additional evidence classes and not claims that every mechanism in the book maps directly to an AI system.

  • Human influence mechanisms for AI builders — systems-level framing for manipulation, persuasion, human agency and decision systems.
  • Manipulation and AI safety — deceptive techniques, vulnerability, optimization, red-team questions and deployment boundaries.
  • Human agency, choice architecture and AI interface design — defaults, friction, transparency, correction and autonomy in HCI.

Limitations

This edition can contain classification errors, incomplete coverage, disputed interpretations and source-selection bias. Research fields change. Some constructs have stronger empirical support than others. The presence of a citation does not establish universal effectiveness, intent, diagnosis, legality or market value.

Readers should use primary literature and professional judgment for high-stakes academic, clinical, legal or safety decisions.
