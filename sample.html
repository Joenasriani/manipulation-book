What this edition is — and is not

This is an independent, source-mapped study edition. It is not a peer-reviewed journal publication, clinical manual, or systematic review. The phrase dark psychology may be used as a reader-facing umbrella term for search and discovery; it is not treated here as a single scientific discipline.

The book organizes documented mechanisms, boundaries, editorial syntheses and hypotheses so that the reader can distinguish what is directly sourced from what is interpretive. AI tools assisted parts of research organization, drafting, checking and production; the edition's claims are intended to remain traceable to cited sources rather than to AI authority.

Why this book exists

Influence becomes easier to examine when it has a name, an observable mechanism and a traceable source. This book joins those elements across everyday conversation, organizations, commerce, relationships, interfaces, information systems and AI. Its purpose is to improve the reader's ability to explain what is happening, distinguish evidence from suspicion and design more accountable applications.

The fool and the wise are positions we can move between, not permanent types of people. The unexamined conclusion is the point of departure. The studied conclusion asks what was said, what was inferred, which alternatives were available and what could change the interpretation.

Evidence and implementation are different questions

E means the source atlas identifies a directly documented mechanism or an authoritative legal or technical category. It does not mean strong evidence of universal effectiveness.

C means the specific label or configuration is an editorial synthesis assembled from documented components. H means the precise configuration remains a hypothesis within the source map.

Class 1 identifies an implementation that can be legitimate under the conditions described. Class 0 groups deceptive, coercive, exploitative or adversarial configurations for analysis. The notation is project metadata, not an assessment of a person's character or a universal legal conclusion.

Chapter 05 · Class 0

Truthful words and false impressions

The sentence can be true while the conclusion is false.

Communication conveys more than literal assertions. Listeners infer relevance, completeness and the reason a fact was selected. Research on paltering examines the active use of truthful statements to mislead; work on communicative norms also shows how misleading impressions can arise without the same intent. The distinction matters because a misleading outcome alone does not establish deliberate deception.

Consider three levels of analysis. First, record the exact statement. Second, describe the conclusion the audience is likely to draw in that setting. Third, investigate whether the speaker knew material context that undermines that conclusion.
